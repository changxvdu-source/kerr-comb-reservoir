Title:
Kerr-microcomb reservoir computing under realistic noise: spectral-phase input masks, the laser-linewidth limit and a calibrated software comparison

Authors:
Changxu Du

Abstract:
Kerr-microcomb reservoir computers have been proposed with several input encodings, but each has been compared with software under different conditions. Using Lugiato-Lefever simulations, we compare eight encodings under one protocol, with identical device ranges, readout, noise model and operating-point budget, and with operating points selected and tested on independent noise realisations. Driving the resonator through a spectral-phase input mask, in which every line of an input comb carries a random complex bias and modulation weight, yields reproducible, coherently seeded dynamics and Mackey-Glass errors 15 to 45 times below a uniformly modulated comb and two to three orders of magnitude below our implementations of single-pump encodings; a linear-cavity control attributes most of this gain to the Kerr term. With ideal detectors about half of the residual error comes from detuning jitter, which a linear readout did not remove even when given the exact jitter sequence, so for Q of about 2x10^6 at 1 GBd the intrinsic laser linewidth must be a few kilohertz or less. Calibrated against echo state networks, the best single-direction resonator matches a 31-node network fed through a converter with about 7 effective bits. Driving both directions of the ring with inverted signals and optimising the mask lowers the error 21-fold (4.5-fold relative to one direction optimised in the same way), to about 8 effective bits against an equally optimised software reservoir of the same readout size. Within the design space explored, the resonator does not surpass software of the same size.
