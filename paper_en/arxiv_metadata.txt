Title:
How far can a Kerr-microcomb reservoir computer go? Spectral-phase input masks, the noise floor and a fair software reference

Authors:
Changxu Du

Abstract:
Kerr-microcomb reservoir computers have been proposed with different encodings, but each has been compared with software under different conditions, so it is unclear how well such a device performs under realistic noise and what limits it. We study this with Lugiato-Lefever simulations under one evaluation protocol: identical device ranges, readout, noise model and operating-point budget, with operating points chosen and tested on independent noise realisations. Feeding the resonator through a spectral-phase input mask, in which every line of an input comb carries a random complex bias and modulation weight, gives reproducible, coherently seeded dynamics and Mackey-Glass errors 15 to 45 times below a uniformly modulated comb and two to three orders of magnitude below our implementations of single-pump encodings; switching off the Kerr term makes the mask 59 times worse without noise and 3.5 times worse with it. With ideal detectors about half of the remaining error comes from detuning jitter, which a linear readout did not remove even when given the exact jitter sequence, so the intrinsic laser linewidth must be a few kilohertz or less for Q of about 2x10^6 at 1 GBd. Calibrated against software, the best single-direction resonator matches a 31-node echo state network fed through a converter with about 7 effective bits, and software stays ahead when the resonator's 31 readout channels are quantized as well. Driving both directions of the ring with inverted signals, reading out both (62 lines) and optimising the mask lowers the error 21-fold relative to that resonator (4.5-fold relative to one direction read out and optimised in the same way), to about 8 effective bits against an equally optimised software reservoir of the same readout size. Within the design space explored, the accuracy of a microcomb reservoir stays below that of software of the same size.
